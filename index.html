Best Practices

These practices help projects stay on schedule and reduce friction for both your client and the production team. They apply to any deliverable type — guides, microsites, video, print.

  • Submit feedback in consolidated review rounds. Multiple back-to-back rounds with small edits each are harder to manage than one consolidated round with all edits at once.
  • If a small number of edits are pending internal approval, consider submitting the completed edits first. Waiting to consolidate everything can delay production unnecessarily.
  • Ensure plan decisions and required content are finalized before kickoff whenever possible. Late-arriving plan changes are the most common source of timeline pressure.
  • Designate a primary reviewer. A single accountable reviewer streamlines approvals and reduces conflicting feedback from multiple stakeholders.
  • Notify your project manager early if timing priorities shift during production. Earlier visibility into changes gives the team more options to absorb them without pushing the in-hand date.

How a successful project looks

A multi-deliverable Open Enrollment project with overlapping pieces: a Custom Benefits Guide (renewal, with print & mail), a Benefits Showcase Microsite, and a Spanish Benefits Guide. The tool schedules them in parallel with one shared pre-production window. Each piece's services (print/mail, translation) are automatically scheduled to meet the same in-hand date.

Custom Benefits Guide (Renewal) — Print & mail required. The tool schedules print/mail production so the deliverable arrives in-hand on the project due date. The renewal designation reduces pre-production friction but uses the same catalog time as new.
Benefits Showcase Microsite — Digital only, runs in parallel with the guide. No print/mail offsets, so the timeline is driven entirely by Active production, Final Client QA & QC, and Approval.
Spanish Benefits Guide — Separate deliverable with translation required. Translation begins after main content is finalized; approximately 5 business days for most deliverables.

Why this works: Deliverables run in parallel across the project. Print/mail and translation timing are scheduled to meet each deliverable's in-hand date. The headline Production Start Date reflects the earliest required production start across everything.

What breaks this: Late content delivery, missed review windows, scope changes mid-flight, or trying to compress timelines without acknowledging reduced flexibility.

Disclaimers & Disclosures

  • This is a scoping tool. Results are estimates. Actual timing depends on scope finalization, content readiness, and client/broker responsiveness.
  • Estimates represent typical maximums. Project completion may be faster with prepared content, responsive client/broker reviews, and minimal scope changes.
  • Calendar excludes weekends and observed 2026/27 federal holidays (both Flimp-observed and broker/client-observed dates).
  • Timeline assumes client/broker review turnarounds within standard windows. One additional business day is added to the project for every business day a milestone is missed.
  • Custom pieces cannot be rushed; standard schedule applies regardless of urgency.
  • Rounds of revisions have no carry-forward value. Unused rounds cannot be banked for future projects or used for additional edits after a project closes. If a project finishes ahead of schedule, that does not create a credit toward later work.
  • Project closure: a project is considered closed when the final distribution email is sent. The final distribution email will not be sent until the client has delivered both final approval and final quality control. After closure, no further edits will be performed on the closed deliverable.
  • New hire versions and alternates after closure: any attempt to convert a closed main deliverable into an alternate (such as a new hire version) requires the account manager to re-scope the work and execute a new statement of work. Project managers cannot perform edits to a closed deliverable, and leftover edits cannot be applied to create an alternate without a new SOW.
  • Tight timelines are possible when the client is ready to be highly responsive, has all required materials and final plan decisions in hand at kickoff, and accepts that not all revision rounds may be used. Coordinate with your account manager to confirm scope feasibility before committing to a compressed schedule.
