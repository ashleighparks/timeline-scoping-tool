Timeline Scoping Tool

About This Tool

This Timeline Scoping Tool provides estimated project timelines based on deliverable type, production requirements, project variables, and standard review assumptions. Timelines reflect estimated active production time and business-day scheduling and are intended to support project planning, coordination, and scheduling.

The tool is designed to provide a realistic planning framework, not a guarantee of delivery. Actual deliverable timelines may vary based on project scope, material readiness, feedback timing, approvals, production requirements, and other specific project factors.

Statement of Work (SOW)

Completing your SOW is the first step toward moving your project into production. The SOW must be finalized and the project approved before the Pre-Production Window begins.

The SOW is not part of pre-production or active production time and should not be used to extend the sales timeline. We recommend finalizing your SOW as early as possible.

Pre-Production

The Pre-Production Window represents a recommended window of at least 10 business days prior to the project start date. This time is used for material gathering, asset gathering, style alignment, project setup, and the kickoff call.

Active production cannot begin until all required project materials have been received and pre-production requirements are complete. Delays in completing these items may impact production start dates and overall timeline estimates.

Pre-Production Requirements

Before active production can begin, the following items should be completed and provided:

  • Required intake materials
  • Branding guidelines
  • Logos and other required assets
  • Style selections and other project-specific creative decisions

Timeline Assumptions

Timeline estimates are based on standard production schedules and may vary depending on project complexity, scope changes, material readiness, and client and/or broker responsiveness. Delays in required materials, feedback, approvals, or final decisions may affect delivery timing.

Important Notes

Results are estimates based on standard project timelines and business-day scheduling. Calendars exclude weekends and observed 2026/27 federal holidays. Custom pieces are not eligible for rush timelines.

Delays in materials, feedback, approvals, or project start may require adjusted timelines, condensed review cycles, or reprioritization of deliverables to support the requested delivery date.

Keeping Your Project on Schedule

Project timelines are a shared responsibility between Flimp and the client. To help keep your project on track:

  • Provide complete project materials and finalized plan information as early as possible.
  • Submit timely, consolidated feedback during each review round.
  • Designate key decision-makers and approvers early in the process.
  • Communicate timeline changes or concerns promptly with your project manager.

Best Practices

These practices help projects stay on schedule and reduce friction for both your client and the production team. They apply to any deliverable type — guides, microsites, video, print.

  • Submit feedback in consolidated review rounds. Multiple back-to-back rounds with small edits each are harder to manage than one consolidated round with all edits at once.
  • If a small number of edits are pending internal approval, consider submitting the completed edits first. Waiting to consolidate everything can delay production unnecessarily.
  • Ensure plan decisions and required content are finalized before kickoff whenever possible. Late-arriving plan changes are the most common source of timeline pressure.
  • Designate a primary reviewer. A single accountable reviewer streamlines approvals and reduces conflicting feedback from multiple stakeholders.
  • Notify your project manager early if timing priorities shift during production. Earlier visibility into changes gives the team more options to absorb them without pushing the in-hand date.

How a successful project looks

A multi-deliverable Open Enrollment project with overlapping pieces: a Custom Benefits Guide (renewal, with print & mail), a Benefits Showcase Microsite, and a Spanish Benefits Guide. The tool schedules them in parallel with one shared pre-production window. Each piece's services (print/mail, translation) are automatically scheduled to meet the same in-hand date.

Custom Benefits Guide (Renewal) — Print & mail required. The tool schedules print/mail production so the deliverable arrives in-hand on the project due date. The renewal designation reduces pre-production friction but uses the same catalog time as new.
Benefits Showcase Microsite — Digital only, runs in parallel with the guide. No print/mail offsets, so the timeline is driven entirely by Active production, Final Client Review, and Approval.
Spanish Benefits Guide — Separate deliverable with translation required. Translation begins after main content is finalized; approximately 5 business days for most deliverables.

Why this works: Deliverables run in parallel across the project. Print/mail and translation timing are scheduled to meet each deliverable's in-hand date. The headline Production Start Date reflects the earliest required production start across everything.

What breaks this: Late content delivery, missed review windows, scope changes mid-flight, or trying to compress timelines without acknowledging reduced flexibility.

Disclaimers & Disclosures

  • This is a project scoping tool; results are estimates based on standard production assumptions. Actual timelines may vary based on project readiness, scope changes, production requirements, and client and/or broker responsiveness.
  • Active production begins after the project kickoff and receipt of all required materials.
  • Estimates are contingent on receiving complete materials, timely feedback, required approvals, and finalized plan decisions by the dates shown. Delays in any of these milestones will extend the project timeline.
  • Translation, print/mail, and alternate-version deliverables will extend the overall project timeline. These extensions must be accounted for in the project scope and timeline view.
  • Project schedules are based on business days only and exclude weekends and observed holidays. Timelines also assume review and approval turnaround times remain within standard project windows.
  • Project closure: a project is considered closed once the final deliverable has been approved, finalized, and distributed. After project closure, additional edits, alternate versions, language versions, or new deliverables may require a new scope review and Change Order. Minor post-finalization updates may be accommodated when feasible.
  • New hire versions and alternates after closure: any attempt to convert a closed main deliverable into an alternate (such as a new hire version) requires the account manager to re-scope the work and execute a new Change Order.
  • Compressed timelines may be possible when all required materials and final plan decisions are available at the start of active production and review feedback is provided promptly. Account managers will work with clients and project management to confirm feasibility and any additional requirements before committing to accelerated schedules.
  • While Flimp makes every effort to meet projected timelines, final delivery dates cannot be guaranteed due to project dependencies, third-party services, and evolving project requirements.
